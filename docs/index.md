# Why this site exists

My admiration for this game comes from a mix of genuine appreciation for its
solid-yet-simple take on action RPG, and nostalgia; this was my first video
game, received as a gift in November 1991 within a week of it hitting the
shelves in the US. I played this game until the batteries blew up in my
original GameBoy, spilling battery acid all over the circuit board and ruining
the device. I got another GameBoy, but then my cartridge was stolen circa 1994.
About a decade later I discovered Ebay and bought 2 copies (ya know, to be
safe). Now I've got an original Square release (not the Sunsoft rerelease)
"Complete in Box" proudly on display in the center of my retro gaming arcade.

In the spring of 1992 I ran into my first glitch in Final Fantasy Adventure
(FFA) by pure luck. I remember being in the Wendel shrine, the mystery man just
whisked away the girl with the Mana pendant, and I was in close pursuit. Until
I wasn't... I glitched into the final dungeon and was killed
near-instantaneously by a jumping ninja. I was awestruck. what on Earth was
that!? At the time, my 7 year-old self thought it must've been an intentional
secret warp, and what could possibly be cooler than sharing this secret through
a letter to Nintendo Power? Alas, I couldn't reproduce it at the time...

Over 30 years later, a new romhack of FFA, called [Legend of the Mana Sword
(LotMS)](https://xenophile127.github.io/Legend-of-the-Mana-Sword/), was being
created by xenophile. I had always wanted to get into rom hacking, and beta
testing for an FFA romhack seemed like a good place to start. Xenophile
introduced me to the great [Mystic
Editor](https://github.com/arathron123/mystic-editor) by arathron, a [marvelous
disassembly](https://daid.github.io/FFA-Disassembly/) by daid (with which this
repo is forked), and the best [Discord server](https://discord.gg/mdTDMKh5FR) a
noob like me could've asked for.  I quickly got pleasantly lost in the
disassembly, started making patches for LotMS, fixing old glitches, and
discovering new glitches. That led me down a diversion exploiting these newly
found glitches for speedrunning. Since then, I've been lucky enough to secure
[a few world records](https://www.speedrun.com/users/dagmor) and had a
blast [discussing strats](speedrun) and cheering on my fellow speedrunners from
all over the globe. The whole community continues to find new glitches to
exploit decades later; it's amazing.

But what about that glitch outside the Wendel shrine!? Well it wasn't until
Christmas Day, 2022, that I finally figured it out. I had been testing another
LotMS release when yet again, I warped from outside the Wendel shrine to the
final dungeon. This time I was ready. I figured out the means to reproduce the
glitch, figured out what caused it, fixed it in a patch, and put to rest the
30-year mystery. Later that day I got another Christmas miracle - a glitched
flying chocobo capable of taking me to new lands left forsaken by the original
developers. I knew then I had to write this up somewhere - a [Glitch
Encyclopedia](glitch) of sorts.

Finally, my romhacking pursuits have led me to multiple projects. I still
continue to support the excellent [LotMS
hack](https://xenophile127.github.io/Legend-of-the-Mana-Sword/), had the honor
to contribute to Ok Impala's [Embers of
Mana](https://www.okimpala.net/embers-of-mana) total conversion hack, and now
have two projects of my own. The [ROM-As-Intended (RAI)](rai) hack (naming
kudos to the D&D community) aims to make a baseline patch to fix all the
lingering glitches at release and also (optionally) provide a set of small
feature enhancements believed to be in-line with the director's original
vision. The [Gemma Knight Edition (GKE)](gke) hack goes above RAI to create a
personal expression of the game, anchored to the original content, but tied to
faster and tighter gameplay, selective balance changes, and a few secrets
scattered throughout for a more engaging and challenging experience.
